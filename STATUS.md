# Status

Version 0.37.1, 16 November 2026. Format version 26, layout rules version 26.

This is a first version. It is meant for trying the idea of a document format that stores exact positions, not yet for daily work.

## What works

**Text**
- Typing, selecting with mouse and keyboard, deleting, undo and redo.
- Cut, copy and paste, also from a right click: plain text from and to other programs; formatting is kept for a paste inside the same document. A picture can be pasted too: a screenshot, a picture copied in another program, or a picture file copied in the Explorer.
- Font, size, bold, italic, underline, strike-through, raised and lowered text, text colour, highlight. Paragraph styles Normal, No spacing (no gap after a paragraph), Title, Heading 1 to 3, Footnote.
- A code look for program text (letters of equal width).
- Copying a format onto other text, and erasing a format.
- Spelling check with the checker and the dictionaries of Windows: unknown words are marked on the screen, and a right click offers other spellings.
- Page setup: paper, upright or on its side, margins. Styles can be changed (font, size, colour, alignment, line spacing, space before and after, indents) and new styles added.
- A table of symbols to put into the text; a symbol the font of the text does not have is put in with a font that has it.
- A character the font of its text does not have (an arrow such as ⇒, a raised letter such as ˣ) gets a font that has it, as text of its own with that font: when a Word document is opened, and when text is typed or pasted into the text of the document. The fonts tried, in order: Cambria Math, Segoe UI Symbol, Segoe UI, Segoe UI Emoji, Microsoft YaHei, Yu Gothic, Malgun Gothic, Nirmala UI, Segoe UI Historic; in a text set in a Segoe font the Segoe ones come first. What is typed after such a character keeps the font of the text. Until 0.23.1 the empty box of the font was shown.
- Lines at the edges of a paragraph: above, below, at both sides, in one thickness and colour, with room between line and text. A paragraph has those of its style, its own, or none. The Paragraph box has a list for them (None, Below, Above, Above and below, All around); a line made there is three quarters of a point thick and black, one that came with a Word file keeps its thickness and colour. Lines at the sides stand outside the text and run on to the next paragraph when it has the same ones, so several paragraphs stand in one frame. Shown on screen, in the PDF and in print (all three draw from the same routine; only screen picture and PDF were looked at). Format version 24, layout rules 22.
- Word documents: the lines Word draws at the edges of a paragraph, from the paragraph or its style, are read as plain lines (a double or dashed line becomes a single one). Paragraphs that follow each other with the same lines stand in one frame as in Word. Thirteen of the 93 Word files on this PC have such lines.
- Not there for these lines: a thickness or colour of their own in the Paragraph box, lines of different thickness at different edges made here, a line between the paragraphs of a frame, lines for a style in the Styles box (a style has them only from a Word file), shading behind a paragraph.
- The room between paragraphs gives a little at the ends of pages, as the room between words does in justified text (since 0.36.0 part of the layout: "Adapt the spacing between paragraphs at page ends" in the Page box; on for new documents and for Word files, off for documents saved before 0.36.0 until it is ticked; stored as `adaptSpacing`, FORMAT.md §6 "Giving room"). Until 0.36.0 this was done once, when a Word file was opened, and wrote the changed rooms into the paragraphs; now the file keeps its own spacing and it works for documents written here and while editing.
  - Only the gaps between paragraphs change (empty paragraphs in a gap come closer together); never the lines, the margins or the room of the footnotes. On a page with pictures that the text keeps clear of, a picture moves with the line that holds it and what stands beside it moves along; nothing moves up into a picture's band (since 0.37.0; in 0.36.0 only what lay below such pictures gave, and a letter with a row of logos near its foot took a second page).
  - Less room: the largest gap first; a gap larger than is usual on its page gives up to two fifths of itself, an ordinary one up to a fifth. Tried where something would go over to the next page, from the largest piece: the rest of the section, the rest of the list, the rest of the paragraph (or a paragraph that is only a picture, an equation or a chart). The room above the piece gives too. Also where a heading or a block that follows does not fit below a hole of a line or more: it comes back with its section.
  - More room: the smallest gap first; up to two fifths, a quarter for a gap less than half the usual, nothing for one more than twice the usual. A paragraph that would be cut begins the next page whole, with the headings that keep with it, if the room above it can fill the hole it leaves (and the paragraph fits on one page). A page whose text goes on, with a hole of a line or more at its foot, is filled down to its lower edge. A page that ends where a section ends is not stretched.
  - The empty paragraph that ends a document never makes a page of its own, also with a page break before it: it stays at the foot of the last page with text (since 0.37.0). So the first Enter at the very end of a full page leaves the caret at its foot; the next Enter, or typing, moves on to the next page.
  - A page gives once at most. Left alone: page breaks made by hand, tables and pieces with tables, a paragraph cut where its writer began a new line by hand, the last page.
  - While typing near the foot of a page, the text above may move by a few points as the gaps change.
  - On the 93 Word files of this PC, against 0.35.0 (the pass at opening): the same number of pages in 92, one page fewer in one (its third page held nothing but an empty paragraph); none has more. Page breaks differ in one other file, where two pages keep one or two more paragraphs.
- Word documents, pictures in the older drawing forms of Windows (EMF and WMF, also packed as EMZ and WMZ) are shown: each is drawn once when the file is opened, at 200 dots to the inch (no side over 3000 dots), and kept in the document as a PNG picture. It is then a picture like any other; it is no longer a drawing that stays sharp at any size. Until 0.28.2 such pictures were left out. Other picture forms (GIF, TIFF, BMP, the HD Photo form) were already kept as PNG.
- Word documents, embedded objects and older drawings (an equation of the old kind, a field of a form, a piece of another program, an older picture shape) are shown as the picture that stood for them in Word, in the line, at the size of their shape. They cannot be opened or changed here; the notes after opening say how many there are. On the 93 Word files of this PC: four files, one of them a form with 84 fields that now shows its boxes. Still left out: drawings and objects that have no picture (two files), shapes and diagrams.
- A Word file that is open in Word at the same time can be opened here (until 0.27.0 this failed with "the file is being used by another process").
- Word documents, header and footer keep clear of the text: the margin at the top or bottom is made larger whenever a header or footer would come nearer to the text than 9 pt, also where Word lets the text end nearer (0.24.1 to 0.26.0 only did this when they reached into the text).
- Word documents, room between paragraphs: Word counts the larger of the room below a paragraph and the room above the next one, not both, unless the document says otherwise (`doNotUseHTMLParagraphAutoSpacing` in its settings; tried with Word itself on three variants of one file). The import now does the same in the text of the document: what the first paragraph has below is taken off what the next one has above. Until 0.24.0 both were counted, which put about 5 to 10 pt too much above every heading.
- Word documents, fonts of the theme: where a style asks for a font of the theme and also names a font, the font of the theme is used, as Word does (until 0.24.0 the named one).
- Word documents, header and footer: one written in a single size keeps that size (it becomes the size of the Header or Footer style), and the margin is made larger only when its lines would reach into the text (until 0.24.0 also when they came within 9 pt of it).
- With these, the Word file this was found on (`latex-test\Facit.*`, three pages) has the same lines on every page as in Word, each within 3 pt of Word's place.
- The font size box is wide enough for sizes with a half point such as 10.5 (it cut off the last digit).
- Fonts are also taken from the folder where Office keeps the fonts of its documents on this PC (`AppData\Local\Microsoft\FontCache\4\CloudFonts`: Aptos, the font of new Word documents, and others), after the installed ones. Until 0.23.1 a document in Aptos was shown in Segoe UI. Layout rules 21 for this; a file saved before is laid out again when opened, with the same result unless it names one of these fonts.
- Keyboard shortcuts that can be changed under Preferences: 31 commands of the program and the 92 symbols of the equation box, each with standard keys or none.
- Kinds of underline: single, double, thick, dotted, dashed, wavy. Changing the case of text: sentence case, small letters, capitals, a capital for each word, each letter switched.
- Sources and citations: a list of sources kept in the document; citations in the text that read as author and year, (Smith, 2020), or as numbers, [1], and follow when the sources change; a list of references made from the cited sources.
- Table of contents: the headings of levels 1 to 3 with their pages, the numbers at the right edge behind a row of dots; written again on the Contents button and before saving, printing and making a PDF.
- Links: text that leads to a web page or an e-mail address, made with the Link button or Ctrl+K, opened with Ctrl and a click. In the PDF they work, the headings are bookmarks and the entries of the table of contents lead to their pages.
- A setup file that installs the program for one user without administrator rights (folder, Start menu, desktop shortcut if wanted, the .docu file type, an entry under Installed apps) and removes it again.
- Tables: cells joined across columns and down rows and split again; shading for a cell, a row, a column or the table; all lines, only those above and below the rows, only those around the table, or none, in three thicknesses; tables that filled the text width follow when the margins change.
- Paragraph box: room above and below, line spacing and the three indents for the selected paragraphs alone; what is left as the style says goes on following the style.
- Boxes: the Box button puts in a frame for text or a picture (a table of one cell); its lines, shading and width are changed like those of a table. Pictures, equations and charts can stand inside table cells and boxes.
- Blocks that stay together: a paragraph can be marked in the Paragraph box as the beginning of a block (a task with its parts). A block of which less than half would fit at the bottom of a page starts on the next page; one that mostly fits stays, and its rest goes on. The Word import marks numbered tasks this way.
- Word documents (.docx) open as new documents, laid out anew: text with its formats, the styles, lists, tables (joined cells, shading, lines), pictures, charts (drawn again from their numbers as bars, lines or a pie), footnotes and endnotes, links, equations, text boxes (as a box of their own if they had a line around them or a background), page breaks, paper and margins, and a simple header and footer with page numbers and with the logo of the header or footer (in its place at the left, in the middle or at the right, at a size that fits into the margin; a margin too narrow for the header or footer is made larger), also those Word shows on the first page only. A paragraph of a Word header or footer is a line here. Texts that would run into each other or into the logo are put one under the other, a line too long goes on in the next line, and the margin is made larger for the lines; only what would need more than a sixth of the page is cut and ends with … (the message says so). What this program has no place for is put where it reads well or left out, and a message says what. The Word file is only read. Tried on a document made by hand and on the 23 templates that come with Word on this PC: all were read, and every result passes the format check.
- No line of a paragraph alone at the bottom or the top of a page (on in new documents, set under Page). Words divided at the ends of lines, for Spanish by its rules for syllables, for English (American spelling) and Swedish by the pattern lists of the TeX hyphenation project, which come with the program in the folder `hyph` with their notices (set under Page); the places are kept in the file.
- Long documents: measured with `--bench` on a document of 742 pages (6100 paragraphs, 2.6 million characters) on the PC it was written on: a typed letter takes about 14 ms until the window has drawn it, opening the file 0.2 s, saving 0.3 s, the PDF 0.6 s. The recovery copy of a document of more than 400 paragraphs is packed and written beside the typing (about 60 ms of it still holds the typing up at that length), and its words are counted when the typing pauses.
- Find and replace, in the text or in the footnote the caret is in. A count of the words of the text.
- Left, centre, right and justified paragraphs; line spacing and left indent for single paragraphs; lists with four kinds of bullets, seven forms of numbers (1. 1) I. A. a) a. i.) and three kinds with several levels (1.1.1., 1. a. i., 1) a) i)), up to nine levels deep; tabs every 1.27 cm; line breaks inside a paragraph; page breaks.
- A4 pages shown one below the other, with zoom. Several documents at once, each in a window of its own.
- A recovery copy of a document with unsaved changes, written a few seconds after the last change and at least every half minute; offered at the next start if the program did not close in the normal way. The user's own file changes only on saving.
- Preferences, kept on this PC: centimetres or inches (centimetres unless changed); light or dark colours for the program, the same as Windows, or a colour of the user's own; a colour for the sheet on the screen, such as natural paper or soft grey, which leaves print and PDF white; whether screenshots and screen recordings of the program are allowed (not allowed unless changed).
- Header and footer with up to three texts each (left, middle, right), each of up to eight lines, with the page number and the number of pages; optionally not on the first page. Each can also hold one picture, such as a logo, at the left, in the middle or at the right: it stands in the line, beside the text of its place, and the program gives it a size that fits into the margin. The first page can have a header and footer of its own (texts and pictures), or none.
- Saving as PDF, written by the program itself: every piece stands where the layout put it, the text can be selected, copied and searched, and of each font only the glyphs used are put into the file (the six-page sample is about 250 KB).
- Printing from a box of the program, which opens at once while the printers are found and asked in the background: a picture of every page, the choice of printer, all pages or pages typed like 1-3, 5, copies, one side or both sides of the sheet where the printer offers it, and the paper.

**Tables**
- Rows and columns of cells with formatted text, typed in place; rows grow with their text; a long table goes on over the pages row by row.
- Rows and columns are added and taken away, from a right click inside the table or with buttons that are shown while the caret is in a table; column widths are changed by dragging; the table stands left, centred or right.

**Images**
- PNG and JPEG, in the line or on a line of their own, resized by dragging a corner, stored inside the document.
- Floating images, dragged to their place: with text above and below only, with text around (on the wider side), behind the text, or in front of it.

**Footnotes**
- Numbered by themselves, typed at the foot of the page with the same formatting as text.
- A line stays on a page only if its footnotes fit there too.

**Equations**
- Written in one of two ways, in two tabs of one box: typed into the drawn equation itself, with shapes whose parts are filled in (fraction, power, index, square root, sum, product, integral, brackets, absolute value), or typed in one line: indices and exponents, fractions, square roots, sums, products and integrals with limits, Greek letters and common symbols, brackets that grow, function names.
- In the line, or on a line of their own in the larger display form.
- LaTeX written over several lines is read as one piece: from a line where `\begin{…}`, `\[` or `$$` opens to the line where it closes, inside one paragraph of a Word document and in pasted text. Each row becomes an equation on its own line. Until 0.23.0 the lines `\begin{aligned}` and `\end{aligned}` stayed in the text. In a Word document where these lines are paragraphs of their own, they still stay.
- The buttons of the format row that show letters (Aa, B, I, U, S, code, LaTeX, x with a raised or lowered 2, the A of the text colour, ab) stand on one line, in the font of the window: each face is one line of the same height (`Letter` in MainWindow.cs). Until 0.28.1 they were in four fonts and at four heights.
- Page numbers in the Page box: a place (none, at the foot or at the top, in the middle, on the right or on the left) and a look (1, Page 1, 1 of 4, Page 1 of 4). The choice writes a line with `{page}` into the header or footer, as the Header box does; other text there stays, and where the place already holds text the number gets a line of its own below it. A margin the header or footer would then reach into is made larger. The Header box shows and changes the same line. Not tried by hand in the window.
- The lists in the boxes Sources and citations, Styles and Preferences (shortcuts) are white boxes with an edge in the light theme (they could not be told from the window).
- Reference systems: the box Sources and citations has a list with APA, Harvard, Chicago (author and date), MLA, IEEE, Vancouver and plain numbers, and shows how a citation looks in the one chosen. The citations in the text and the list of references are written as the system has them: which names stand in a citation and how they are joined, whether the year is in it, how authors are written in the list (initials or given names, turned or straight), where the year stands, the heading (References, Reference list, Works Cited), and the words before a web address. A source has authors, year, title, where it appeared and a web address; the systems are followed as far as these go, so volume, issue, pages, edition, the kind of source and the day it was read are not there, and every title is set slanted (in Vancouver upright) whatever kind of work it is. Page numbers in a citation, as MLA has them, are not there. Format version 25, layout rules 23. Looked at: the box, and the sample page for Chicago.
- The list of styles in the format row shows every style in its own look (font, weight, slant, underline, colour, and a size that follows the style's between 11.5 and 16), and shows a style that was changed as it is now; on a dark window a dark colour is shown light. The button that opens the Styles box is a blue pencil right behind the list (it was the sign ¶).
- Styles from the list in the format row: with words selected inside a paragraph (not all of it), only those words take the look of the chosen style (font, size, weight, slant, underline, colour) and the paragraph keeps its own style, as in Word (until 0.32.0 the whole paragraph got the style). The words get a character style that belongs to the paragraph style (its id with "Char" behind it, shown as "Heading 1 (words)"), and when the paragraph style is changed in the Styles box they change with it (since 0.32.2). Without a selection the style goes to the paragraph of the caret; a whole paragraph selected, or a selection over several, gives those paragraphs the style. Words that only look like a heading are not a heading for the table of contents.
- Update a style to match the text (since 0.35.0), as Word's "Update Heading 1 to Match Selection": a right click in the text, or on the list of styles, offers "Update “Heading 1” to match" for the style of the text at the caret (greyed when the text already looks as its style says). The style takes the font, size, weight, slant, underline and colour of the text at the caret, or at the start of the selection, and, for the paragraph's own style, the paragraph's own alignment, spacing, indents and lines; all text of the style follows. In the selected paragraphs what was set by hand and now only repeats the style is let go. For words that carry the look of a style (see above) that style is the one updated. It can be undone, and so can a change made in the Styles box (until 0.35.0 it could not).
- A new document no longer shows "New document." at the foot of the window.
- Dark page: a small switch at the foot of the window, at the right (a half-dark disc), makes the sheet dark and shows what stands on it light. Every colour of the document is shown with its lightness turned round and its kind kept: black text becomes light, a dark blue heading a light blue, a yellow highlight a dark yellow, white the colour of the sheet. Pictures are not changed (one with a white background stays a white box). It is for the screen only: the document, the PDF and the print stay as they are. The choice is kept and holds for every window. The same turning of colours now also happens when a paper colour of the user's own under Preferences is dark, which was unreadable before. Seen in one window picture of the self-test (text, a picture, a chart); equations, tables and long documents were not looked at in it.
- A spelling button at the foot of the window, at the left: it names the language that is checked and, when there are any, how many words the check does not know, in the text, the table cells and the footnotes together ("Spanish (Spain) · Spelling: 12"); with none it shows the language alone. Beside it, while there are such words, two small arrows lead to the word to check before the caret and to the next one (in the text of the document, going round at its ends). A click on the button opens a menu: the languages Windows can check on this PC (eight here), "As Windows", and "Off". The choice holds for every window and is kept, the same setting as under Preferences. The words are counted a few paragraphs at a time, 0.4 seconds after the last change to the text, so the number follows the typing with a short delay (until 0.31.2 it was only counted anew in very long documents, so in an ordinary one it never changed; the self-test now types a word into the window and waits for the number). Seen in the window picture of the self-test; the menu was not opened by hand.
- Shortcuts were gone through for keys with two meanings (0.30.3). None found among the standards: 38 commands and 92 signs all have different keys, no command has keys of a sign, none has keys the page keeps for itself (copy, cut, paste, select all, the keys for raised and lowered numbers, moving by words), and none is Ctrl with Alt and a key that types a character on a common keyboard (the right Alt key counts as Ctrl with Alt; Ctrl+Alt+E is the euro sign). A self-test check now keeps it so. Two things were changed: with Shift, the keys B, I, U, L, E, R, J, A, X, Y and Space no longer do what they do without Shift (Ctrl+Shift+B used to make bold too, a hidden second meaning); and when a newer version gives a command standard keys that the user had already given to something else, the user's choice stays and the command has no keys until it is given some.
- The LaTeX button shows the mode the moment it is switched, also by its keys Ctrl+Shift+E (until 0.30.1 the keys switched the mode but the button only showed it after the next change in the text, so it looked as if the keys did nothing). Checked in the window of the self-test; the keys themselves cannot be pressed by the self-test.
- A small menu beside the LaTeX button chooses where equations made from LaTeX stand: in the line (what holds unless something else is chosen, since 0.30.1), on a line of their own, or as it comes (LaTeX alone on a line gets a line of its own, other LaTeX stands in the line). It holds for typed and pasted LaTeX and for every document until it is chosen anew (not kept after the program is closed). An equation that is selected when the choice is made takes that place at once. The same two places are in the menu of the right mouse button on an equation, as before.
- LaTeX mode, for LaTeX that is typed: the LaTeX button in the format row (Ctrl+Shift+E) switches it on and off like bold. While it is on, what is typed stands as text in the character style "LaTeX" (Consolas, blue). Enter makes equations of the LaTeX in the paragraph and leaves the caret right behind them, so that the line can be written on (since 0.30.1; before, it also began a new paragraph); where there is no LaTeX to convert, Enter begins a new paragraph as always. The mode stays on after Enter; switching the mode off does the same for the paragraph of the caret. LaTeX that is all the paragraph holds becomes an equation on its own line, LaTeX inside a sentence one inside the line. What is not understood stays as typed, in its style, to be corrected. With text selected, the button makes an equation of the selected LaTeX and leaves the mode as it is. One undo gives the typed text back. Outside the mode, typing is ordinary text again. Tried through the editor in the self-test, not with the keyboard in the window; moving the caret away from unfinished LaTeX does not convert it (Enter, the button, or selecting it does).
- Mathematics written as LaTeX becomes equations, in a Word document that is opened, and in text that is pasted into the text of the document while the LaTeX mode is on (since 0.29.1; before, pasting converted whether the button was pressed or not, which made the button look as if it did nothing). Without the mode, pasted LaTeX stays the text it is; selecting it and pressing the button converts it. A line that is nothing but LaTeX becomes an equation on a line of its own; LaTeX between `$ … $` or `\( … \)` becomes an equation inside the line, between `$$ … $$` or `\[ … \]` one on a line of its own. Read are fractions, roots, exponents and indices, sums, integrals and limits, Greek letters and symbols, brackets (also with `\left`, `\right`, `\big`), `\text`, spaces, and the rows of an aligned set (one equation for each row). A price such as $5, a file path and LaTeX with a command the program does not know stay text. A paragraph of a Word document that became nothing but equations and was set as program code takes the size and the spacing of ordinary text. Tried on a test file made for it (`latex-test\Latex.docx`) and on two real Word files with 46 such lines each; in the other 91 Word files tried on this PC it changed nothing.
- In the typed line, text between two quotation marks is written upright as it stands (for units and words).
- In an equation that Word wrote, what stands directly before or behind a fraction stays outside of it (until 0.22.0 a number before a fraction was read into its upper part), and an exponent on a fraction stands on the whole fraction.

**Charts**
- Bars, lines and pie from a small table of up to six series; title, names for the horizontal and the vertical axis (along the axes, the vertical one turned, or at their ends), and legend; in the line or on a line of their own; resized like images.
- Graphs of formulas in x, one or several in one chart (one formula in each line of the box; a line can begin with a name for its graph). The points are worked out when the graph is made and kept in the document as whole numbers.

**The file**
- Save and open `.docu`. The stored layout is shown as it is when the fingerprints of content, layout and fonts match; otherwise the program says what differs and lays the document out again.
- A choice under Preferences that makes Windows open `.docu` files with the program and show them with the icon of a document (for the user who presses it; not tried: the self-test only checks what would be written).
- Only `.docu` documents are opened. A document in the format of an earlier version (`.udoc`, `.rks`, `.rwd`) is refused with a message that says so; `--resave` of that earlier version was the way to bring one over.

**Checks**
- The self-test has 314 checks and all pass. They cover: the same positions from two layouts and after save and reopen, for eleven sample documents (up to 61 pages); the stored layout coming back byte for byte; a changed font fingerprint being noticed; 700 random edits each followed by a comparison with a layout from scratch; undo and redo returning the exact documents; the structure of 20 typed equations; find, replace, copying and erasing formats, and the word count; the preferences being kept and read back; the window being out of screenshots unless they are allowed; the window turning dark and back to light while open.

- `tools\check_format.py`, a separate program written from FORMAT.md alone, recomputes the layout of all sample documents (4,411 lines, 202 images, equations and charts, 369 table cells and 32 header and footer texts in thirteen files) and finds no difference from the stored positions. Not reached by any sample, and so not confirmed by it: indents other than 0, justified lines that contain a tab, the fallback when a font or a style of it is missing, symbol fonts, a paragraph's own spacing or indent inside a footnote, graphs whose formulas have different numbers of points, and the three things that are only drawn and not stored as positions: underline, strike-through and highlight.
- Documents made by chance (`--random`, since 0.37.0): 1,900 of them, with every kind of content and mostly with the room between paragraphs giving, pass the program's own checks; the first 400 also agree with `tools\check_format.py` line by line. The first run found one fault, fixed in 0.37.1: a picture that pushes the text below it could come up into the paragraph above it when the room gave.

- `tools\check_pdf.py` opens the PDFs of ten sample documents with a separate PDF engine (PyMuPDF): all open without repair, their text can be read out, and each of about 122,000 compared characters stands within 0.005 point of the place the layout gives it. Pages of the sample were also drawn by that engine and looked at.

## What does not work, or is not there

**Not in this version at all**
- Saving Word files. Word files are opened and laid out anew; nothing is written back as .docx.
- A box to choose or change character styles. They exist and are stored (Code, LaTeX, and the styles that give words the look of a paragraph style), but only these are set by the program.

**Limits of what is there**
- No kerning, ligatures or right-to-left text: one character gives one glyph. A character that is in none of the fonts tried for it is shown as the font's empty box. In a file saved before 0.24.0 such a character keeps its empty box until something is typed in its paragraph.
- Find works inside one paragraph at a time, and only on text: it does not look into equations or charts. Copy format carries the look of the characters, not the alignment or spacing of the paragraph.
- A footnote is never split between two pages, and footnotes hold text only.
- Header and footer are plain lines in one format (the styles Header and Footer), without bold words, tables or links; they stand in the margin and do not push the text, so a header of many lines needs a larger margin (the Header box asks; that question was not tried by hand). Setting them is not a step for undo. A picture in them cannot be taken hold of on the page: its place and height are set in the Header box, which was not tried by hand. There is one header and one footer for the whole document and one pair for the first page; none for even and odd pages or for single sections, and no shapes in them.
- PDF: text stays real text for fonts with TrueType outlines whose licence allows embedding. Text in other fonts (fonts with PostScript outlines, or with a licence that forbids embedding) is written as outlines: same look and place, but it cannot be selected. The PDF has no bookmarks, no links and no document properties, and it was checked with one PDF engine only.
- Printing: Windows cannot embed every font in a print job; on this PC not Calibri and not Cambria. Text in such a font is printed as outlines, which look the same and stand at the same places. For a PDF use the PDF button, not the PDF printer. The pages are A4: on other paper a page stands in the middle and is made smaller if it does not fit; it is never made larger, and landscape is not offered. Both sides are offered only when the printer says it can; turning the sheets by hand is not supported. The choices are what the printer reports to Windows, so a printer that reports nothing gets A4 on one side. Printing to a real printer has not been tried; the pages were only sent through the same path into a file and looked at.
- Tables: a cell holds text, pictures, equations and charts, but no footnotes, no citations, no pictures that float and no table inside a table; a picture in a cell is selected and resized with the mouse like any other, but a right click on it gives the menu of the table, not that of the picture; cells are joined one neighbour at a time (to the right or below), not by selecting several; which lines are drawn and how thick they are is set for the whole table, not for a single cell or edge, and lines are always black; the shadings are seven fixed colours; a table without lines shows no guide lines on the screen; a row is never split between two pages, and the rows a joined cell fills stay on one page, so a joined block taller than a page runs over the bottom; a selection cannot run from one cell into another; at most 12 columns. Find does not look into table cells unless the caret is in one, and then only into that cell.
- Lists: a numbered list always starts at 1; it cannot continue an earlier list or start at another number. The bullets and number forms are the fixed ones of the list menu.
- Citations: a source has five fields only (authors, year, title, where it appeared, web address), so the reference systems are followed as far as these go. There are no page numbers inside a citation, and every title is set slanted (upright in Vancouver) whatever kind of work it is.
- Word import, tried on real files: `tools\import_check.py` read all 93 Word documents found on this PC outside other projects (templates of Word, documents of the user, files other programs had left). All 93 were read; none has anything outside the width of the text, an empty page, or a picture wider than its place. In 60 of them Word had written its own number of pages: 34 have the same number here, 22 have more pages here and 4 fewer. Found and mended on the way: a file whose main part has another name was not read; an empty paragraph did not have the height Word gives it; paragraphs Word sets close together (the items of a list) had room between them; a table with more columns than fit had words cut letter by letter. Such a table now gets its columns shared out by what stands in them, and smaller text (not below six tenths) if that is not enough. First pages of eight of the results were looked at; the others only measured.
- Word documents: lines and pages do not break where Word broke them, and nothing is written back as .docx. A picture that stood freely stands beside the text on the side Word had it, or on a line of its own; where exactly, the layout decides. A text box stands behind its paragraph, not where Word had it; one without a line around it or a background becomes ordinary text. A chart keeps its numbers, names and titles but gets the look of this program, and kinds this program does not have (stacked or lying bars, areas, points, rings, more than one kind in one chart) become plain bars, lines or a pie; no chart made by Word itself was tried, only one written by hand. Shapes, SmartArt, pictures in EMF or WMF form, comments and what tracked changes had deleted are left out. Table cells and text boxes hold no footnotes and no tables, so footnotes inside them are left out and a table there becomes lines of text; pictures, charts and equations there are kept, standing in the line and no wider than their place. In a footnote only text is kept. Equations: fractions, roots, powers, indices, sums and integrals with limits, brackets and function names are taken over; matrices, accents and lines of equations are written more simply. Character styles become plain formats of the text. Columns, tab stops, borders and shading of paragraphs, numbering that goes on across lists, and different headers for different sections are not taken over. Opening a .docx in the window, with its message, was not tried by hand, and one document written by a person was tried (a worksheet with numbered tasks and equations); it showed three faults that are mended: equations now have the size of the text around them, signs of Word that are not meant to be seen are no longer drawn, and the number of a list stands where Word had it, also at the very edge of the text. Its numbered tasks are kept together as blocks.
- Links: only web and e-mail addresses are opened; there are no links to a place inside the document other than the entries of the table of contents. The mouse pointer does not change over a link and no hint shows its address; a right click does. An address typed into the text does not become a link by itself. Opening a link and the link box were not tried by hand.
- Dividing words: Spanish, English (American spelling) and Swedish. A further language needs its list of patterns in the folder `hyph`. The English list divides by American usage; British spelling has a list of its own that is not there. Swedish compound words are not always divided at the joint. The language is one for the whole document, so a passage in another language is divided by the wrong rules. Headings, titles and the table of contents are never divided. There is no limit on how many lines in a row end with a hyphen, no optional hyphen to type by hand, and no way to keep one word whole. Words with a prefix are divided by sound (su-brayar), which the Spanish rules allow but some prefer otherwise. Tried on made-up text and single words only.
- Lines alone on a page: the rule looks at lines of the full width only, so beside a floating picture a line can still stand alone; a paragraph of two or three lines moves to the next page whole.
- Blocks: the mark is set by hand or by the Word import, not by this program's own numbered lists. The Word import takes a paragraph for a task when it is a numbered item of the first level or begins with a number typed by hand ("4. Solve"); it can miss tasks written otherwise, and it needs at least two to mark any. Empty paragraphs at the end of a block stay on the page before when the block moves on.
- Setup: tried only as a trial run into a folder (files, shortcuts, the list of registry entries, removal). A real install, the entry under Installed apps and removing from there were not tried. The setup file is not signed, so Windows may warn before it starts. It does not bring the .NET 10 Desktop Runtime with it. It is for 64-bit Windows on Intel or AMD processors.
- Table of contents: there is no choice of levels or looks. Between two updates it can be out of date. What is typed into it by hand is lost at the next update. Pasting a picture was tested with made-up pictures only, not with the clipboard of Windows.
- Underline has no colour of its own, and its kinds restart at every change of format. Changing the case goes letter by letter, so a letter that would turn into two (as the German sharp s in capitals) stays as it is.
- Shortcuts: the hints on the toolbar buttons still name the standard keys after a change. Cut, copy, paste, select all, the keys for raised and lowered text and the keys for moving the caret cannot be changed. A command has one set of keys. Symbol shortcuts work in the equation box only.
- Spelling: one language at a time for the whole program, not a language per document or per paragraph; only languages whose dictionary is installed in Windows (on this PC English, Spanish and Swedish); no grammar check. The page and the styles are not steps for undo. A table keeps its column widths when the margins change. A page on its side is printed on paper on its side; this was not tried on a printer.
- Recovery copy: it protects against a crash or a power cut, not against a broken disk or a deleted file (that needs a backup). What was typed in the last few seconds before the stop can be missing. Only the newest copy is offered at a start; others wait for the next start. The offer after a real crash was not tried: the self-test only writes a copy, finds it and opens it.
- Floating images: text passes on one side only; only images can float, not equations or charts; an image is moved by dragging, not with the keyboard; after a drop the image can land a little away from where it was let go, because the text around it is laid out again. Its item in the text is invisible and can be deleted like a character.
- Graphs of formulas: the y axis is set by the values alone, so a formula that shoots up (such as tan x) flattens the rest.
- Equations, typing into the equation: a part that is still empty is not marked, the caret gets there with Tab, the arrow keys or a click; Greek letters with Alt work in the equation box only, not in the text of the document; no selection, no copy and paste, and no undo inside the box (Cancel gives the equation back as it was); a power or an index typed after a fraction belongs to its denominator unless the fraction is put in brackets; the caret cannot stand between a letter and its index or exponent. A wide equation is shown smaller to fit the box. No matrices, no roots other than square roots, no accents; `|x|` bars do not grow; large brackets are the normal bracket drawn bigger, so they get heavier. Equations need the font Cambria Math.
- Charts: no values written on the bars, long labels are not shortened, a pie uses the first series only.
- Images, equations and charts copied to another document arrive as plain text.
- LaTeX: accents (arrows of vectors, hats, bars) and bold or script letters are left out and what they stand on is kept; matrices and cases are written in one line; the rows of an aligned set are not lined up at their signs; a root other than the square root is written as a power. LaTeX that stands inside a sentence without marks around it stays text. In a table cell or a box the equations stand in the line; pasted into a table cell or a footnote, LaTeX stays text. Pasting was tested through the program's own paste, not with the clipboard of Windows, and there is no paste that leaves LaTeX alone (Undo takes the whole paste back).
- Fonts are named by fingerprint, not stored in the file. On a computer with different font files the document is laid out again and may break lines and pages differently.
- Text typed with an input method editor (for example for Chinese or Japanese) appears only when a word is finished.

- Colours: on the dark page pictures keep their own colours, so an image with a white background stays a white box on the dark sheet.
- Colours: "Same as Windows" is read when the program starts and when Preferences is closed with OK; a change in Windows while the program is open is not followed. In dark, only the main window, the Preferences box and the chart box were looked at; the menus, the sheet of colours, the find bar and the other boxes were not.
- Screenshots and colours: the choices cover the program's own windows, which now include its messages and questions and the print box. The boxes for opening and saving a file and for choosing any colour belong to Windows: they keep the look of Windows and are not kept out of captures.

**Not measured**
- Very long documents. A 61-page document lays out in about 20 ms, but every change places all pages again, so documents of thousands of pages are expected to feel slow while typing.

**Not covered by the self-test**
- Work with the mouse (selecting, resizing, clicking into a footnote) and typing into the equation and chart boxes. The self-test opens the window and both boxes and checks that they draw, but it does not click or type in them.
